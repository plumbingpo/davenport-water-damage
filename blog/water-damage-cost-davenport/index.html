How Much Does Water Damage Cost in Davenport?

Home / Blog / How Much Does Water Damage Cost in Davenport?

The Quick Answer

In Davenport, the usual water damage bill lands in a range of $1,500 to $10,000 or more. A minor job involving clean water may cost less than roughly $1,500. At the other end, heavy flooding or sewage with reconstruction work can go well beyond $10,000.

None of this is a quote, only the usual range across the country. Local labor rates and material prices in Davenport, IA move the numbers up or down. The only way to get an exact figure is an on-site inspection with moisture readings.

Pricing by Category of Water

The first thing a restoration estimate looks at is what kind of water it is. There are three categories in the industry standard, and each reflects a level of contamination. A job with dirty water calls for extra protective gear, more disinfecting and more material taken out.

Nationally, the category puts cleanup at about $3 to $7.50 for each square foot. Typical figures look like this.

CategoryWhat it isTypical cost per sq ft
Category 1 (clean water)Supply lines, faucets, water heater tanksAbout $3 to $4
Category 2 (gray water)Washing machine or dishwasher discharge, sump pump failuresAbout $4 to $6.50
Category 3 (black water)Storm surge, flooding rivers, sewageRoughly $7 to $7.50

Category can change. Clean water that sits for a couple of days picks up bacteria and is then treated as gray or black water. It is part of why delay makes a job more expensive.

The Main Price Factors

The same kind of pipe failure may cost one homeowner much more than another. These are the items a contractor weighs in an estimate.

  • Area affected. Labor, equipment and time on site all grow with the amount of wet square footage.
  • Water category. Jobs with clean water cost the least. Nothing costs more to deal with than sewage.
  • How long it sat. Water spreads into subfloors and wall cavities over time. Catching a leak within an hour is much cheaper than discovering it when you return from a weekend away.
  • Materials. Drying goes smoothly on tile and on concrete. Hardwood, carpet pad, drywall and insulation often need special drying or replacement.
  • Mold. Once mold is growing, the scope expands to include remediation, along with air filtration and containment.
  • Reconstruction. One part of the bill is drying. Replacing drywall, flooring, cabinets and paint afterward is a separate one, and on large jobs it is often the bigger of the two.

Estimates usually cover a pair of phases, extraction of the water and then drying of the structure. Drying usually takes 3 to 5 days.

Many companies charge for each day the air movers and dehumidifiers run. Three days of drying is cheaper than six.

Cost by Common Job

These are typical national ranges for the jobs we see most often in Davenport. Treat them as a starting point, not a price.

  • Burst pipe. Cleaning up and drying out often runs $1,500 to $5,000, with repair of the burst pipe billed in addition. The total climbs when water flowed for hours or reached a second floor.
  • Appliance leak. Found early, a leak from a dishwasher, water heater or washing machine is often $1,000 to $4,000. Hidden damage makes a slow leak beneath a cabinet more expensive.
  • Basement flood. Depth, where the water came from and whether the room is finished put most jobs at $2,000 to $10,000 or more. Finished basements land near the top of the range for basement water removal.
  • Sewage backup. Many jobs cost $3,000 to $10,000, some more. Sewage cleanup is Category 3 work, so porous materials are removed and everything is disinfected.
  • Roof leak. Without the roof repair itself, drying and fixing walls, insulation and ceilings is often $1,000 to $5,000.

Will Insurance Pay for Water Damage in Davenport?

It depends on the cause and on your policy. Standard homeowners policies often cover damage that is sudden and accidental, such as a pipe that bursts or a supply line that fails without warning.

Gradual leaks are usually not covered. An insurer may treat a fitting that dripped for months as a maintenance problem. Unless you have a separate flood policy, water that enters from outside, like storm surge or a rising river, is usually excluded. For sewer backup, a dedicated add-on is often required.

Check your policy or call your agent before you assume anything. Remember to consider your deductible. If the work is minor, its cost may be below your deductible and there may be little point in a claim.

How to Keep the Bill Down

Most of the cost of water damage is decided in the first day or two. Some basic actions go a long way.

  • Act fast. Water keeps spreading until it is removed. Every hour adds wet material.
  • Shut off the water. Know where your main valve is and close it as soon as you find a leak.
  • Document everything. Take photos and video before anything is moved or thrown out. Save every receipt.
  • Call a pro before mold starts. It can take as little as 24 to 48 hours for mold to start. Once it does, the job gets longer and more expensive.
  • Move what you can. Get rugs, furniture and boxes off wet floors if it is safe.

If the power has not been cut, never wade into standing water, and avoid any water that could carry sewage. No saving is worth a shock or an infection. If the water came from outside, leave flood cleanup to a crew with the right protective gear.

Turning a Range Into an Estimate

Averages from across the country are fine for planning and still cannot describe your home. To learn the true spread of the water, and the work required to dry and fix it, a technician has to inspect with a meter.
